Service charges: the number that eats your yield

A 7% gross yield with an AED 25 per square foot service charge is not a 7% investment. Service charges are the single most under-read number in Dubai, and the one most likely to turn a good-looking deal into an average one.

We do not publish service charges, and here is why

Dubai's service charges live in Mollak, the DLD system that approves and collects them. Mollak is open to registered jointly-owned-property management companies, auditors and banks. It is not open data, and the per-property lookup behind it is a keyed e-service rather than a published dataset.

We could scrape approximate numbers. We will not, because a wrong service charge changes an investment decision by more than a wrong price does.

Use the official tool instead. DLD publishes a Service Charge Index where you can look up the RERA-approved rate for a specific project and year: Service Charge Index →. It is free and it is authoritative.

If we gain proper Mollak access, browsable service charges and true net yields appear here, and we will say so loudly.

Rules of thumb Dubai investors actually use

AED 10–14 per square foot a year is low and healthy. AED 14–18 is normal for a mid-market tower. Above AED 18–25 you are paying for amenities, and above AED 30 you should have a very good reason. Prime landmark towers run far higher.

On a 1,000 sqft apartment, the difference between AED 12 and AED 25 a foot is AED 13,000 a year. On a 6% gross yield at AED 1.5M, that is more than a fifth of your income.

These are ranges investors quote, not our measurements. Check the official index for your specific building before you commit.
